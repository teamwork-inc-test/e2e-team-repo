## The Podcast/Interview as Sales Motion

The breakthrough: the demo call, the marketing content, and the product onboarding are the same event. You interview an expert about their expertise. That conversation becomes:

1. **Their installable perspective** (the product — they get a free account with it already in it)
2. **A podcast episode / content piece** (marketing — published with permission)
3. **A live demo of the product** (proof — the interview itself shows the product working)

Three outputs from one conversation. This is a content flywheel that also builds the marketplace supply side.

Tanya: "I could build out the marketplace by basically creating a podcast where I interview people about their business and perspective, and each of those things gets packaged into an installable expertise and they're just available."

Freya's reaction to the demo walkthrough confirmed this: the human moment (the call, the expert's reaction to seeing their words cited) sells better than any screen recording.

## Tier Upgrade Path (Crystallized)

The interview produces a perspective in a trial account. The upgrade path is natural behavioral steps:

1. **Interview → trial with perspective → Free** (you got interviewed, here's your perspective, free forever)
2. **Want to add more → Expert** ($81/month — you want to grow it with new materials, new thinking)
3. **Want team collaboration → Team** ($400/month — your team works from your perspective, cited)
4. **Want full engagement → Organization** ($4K/month — we do the distillation, multiple roles, on-prem)

Each tier is a natural next step, not a paywall. The interview seeds the perspective; the user decides how far to take it.

## Hero Video Format (Freya's Design Insight)

Freya: Don't make people read text in a video. The hero video is a human moment, not a screen recording.

The format:
1. You on a call with someone (10 seconds of real conversation)
2. Brief clip showing the perspective being created
3. The output citing the person's words (the screen showing citations)
4. The expert's reaction — "whoa, that's my methodology"
5. Brief statement of what they now have and what their team/students/clients can do with it

This can be assembled from existing Tess call footage if video exists, or from a new short recording.

Key insight: "The human moment sells, not the text." Tina's "it just looks real" applied to video, not just GIFs.

## Demo Script (Finalized)

Using Tess / Wren Learning Partners as the scenario:

1. **"Just got off the phone with Tess @ wrenlearning.example. She told me that program directors are afraid to use AI because they don't trust it to use reliable sources. Can you explain in a short paragraph how her program directors can get AI to cite her methodology when working with teachers?"** → AI asks for a brain dump (the problem — everyone recognizes this)

2. **"Write the intro paragraph for the SOW to turn her training docs for new interns into installable perspectives, so her intern's work can cite her words, aswritten. Annotate your work."** → 100% cited output. Every claim traces to Tess's actual methodology from the onboarding call. Footnotes visible.

3. **"What are we missing about her market?"** → Gap analysis. Three specific gaps with severity. The product knows what it doesn't know.

4. **"Here's the call transcript, can you add her experiences to our perspective?"** → Paste transcript. Memory saved. Perspective grows.

The `, aswritten` at the end of prompt 2 is the mode switch — it tells the AI to use real sources, not make things up. The viewer learns what this means by seeing the output change.

## Tanya's Articulation of the Product (from the call)

Key quotes from Tanya during the Freya call:

On the reframe: "I've let go of the memory word sort of entirely and I'm now calling it installable expertise. Because what we need and the big narrative foundation is not intelligence is capability. And more information does not solve your problem."

On the moat: "The real differentiator and the technical moat is that I take textual documents and I extract perspective from them and then I make that perspective citable in the individual task."

On the market: "The target market is becoming an expert that currently packages their work in a static and bounded way. Are you producing a book, a blog, a set of trainings, a set of courses? Do you deliver gigantic decks that are the deliverable of your perspective? And then importantly, do people then use these things to work from?"

On delegation: "It's really about the delegation of work that requires expertise to carry it out. And the person, the business owner, the consultant, the expert, is currently the bottleneck."

On the Apple analogy: "I'm more interested in developing the Apple to installable expertise than I am in giving you a toolbox."

On the network approach: "You know, I could have some initially available perspectives. Could I get all the people I know to contribute their expertise as the first modules? Like do your design thinking version and have that available."
